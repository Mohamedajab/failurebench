# Literature audit (2 October 2026)

This is a targeted search, not a systematic review. Searches covered missing-feature conformal prediction, mask-conditional validity, adaptive missingness, adversarial robustness, and augmentation dependence. A lack of a search hit is not proof of novelty.

| Primary source | Established contribution relevant here | Effect on this project's scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Zaffran et al., ICML 2023: Conformal Prediction with Missing Values](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zaffran23a.html) | Missing-data augmentation targets coverage conditional on missingness patterns. | Missingness-aware calibration is established; our initially complete-data mask-matched baseline is only a simple special case. |
| [Fan et al., AISTATS 2026: Mask-Conditional Conformal Prediction](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/fan26a.html) | A correction/reweighting framework treats general missing-data mechanisms. | We cannot claim first handling of input-dependent missingness. These methods target a specified mechanism; our audit targets simultaneous finite-family protection and transfer between policies. Their full algorithms are not reproduced here. |
| [Ghosh et al., 2023: Probabilistically robust conformal prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16360) | Quantile-of-quantile calibration trades worst-case protection for protection against most perturbations. | Robust coverage/efficiency tradeoffs are established. Our exhaustive finite family is a different, deliberately limited test setting. |
| [Jeary et al., Pattern Recognition 2025: Verifiably robust conformal prediction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2025.112051) | Verification supplies conformal protection against bounded additive adversarial perturbations for regression and classification. | Robust conformal prediction is established; no first robustness theorem is claimed. We use explicit discrete masks, so no neural verification solver is needed. |
| [Massena et al., 2025: Efficient Robust Conformal Prediction via Lipschitz-Bounded Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05434) | Efficient score bounds and audits address adversarial conformal reliability. | Our rank/max argument is an application of known machinery; finite-mask auditing alone may be too incremental for a methods paper. |
| [Azizi, 2026: Conformal Calibration for Multi-Modal Regression with Missing Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07795) | Disagreement scaling and availability stratification address interval reliability with missing modalities. | Scaling/mask-aware widths are not novel in general. Our per-mask scale is an ablation, not a novelty claim. |
| [You et al., September 2026: Missingness-Aware Conformal Prediction Under Cross-Hospital Distribution Shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30781) | Distinguishes pooled/group/site evaluation and investigates reversals in apparent improvement. | Evaluation targets and aggregation need explicit definitions; we report dataset-wise results and failure boundaries. We do not make clinical claims. |

Candidate contribution: a portable, label-transparent benchmark of fixed-mask, policy-specific, and simultaneous coverage under feature removal, paired with a counterexample and independently normalized max-score baseline. Before manuscript drafting, an expert should review the distinction from robust CP and mask-conditional work, and the strongest missingness methods should be reproduced for any claimed state-of-the-art comparison. The current project is an initial research artifact, not a verified novel algorithm or accepted paper.
